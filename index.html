How a Free Consultation Works

The process is intentionally simple, and most of the preparation is just deciding what you would like to talk through.

Nothing about it is designed to rush you into a decision before you have had a chance to think it over properly.

1. 1. Describe Your Situation

Before the consultation begins, you will be asked to describe what’s going on, a relationship, a decision, a period of transition. There is no need to have it perfectly organized. A general description is enough for a reader to begin working with, and you can add detail as the conversation develops rather than front-loading everything at once.

2. 2. Connect With a Reader

You are paired with a reader suited to your situation. Different readers work through different approaches, from a general clarity session to a more structured chart-based overview, so the tone can vary, though the intent stays consistent: a considered, honest response to what you’ve described, rather than a generic script applied to every visitor.

3. 3. Discuss What Comes Up

The consultation itself is a conversation, not a monologue. The reader shares what they are noticing and why, and you are able to ask questions or clarify anything that feels unclear before the session ends. You are also free to slow the conversation down at any point if something needs more time.

There is no obligation to book a further session once the consultation is over, and no charge for this first conversation. Some people find what they need in that single conversation. Others choose to continue with a longer session afterward. Both are reasonable outcomes to arrive at.

If the consultation raises more than it resolves, that is a normal result too. A single conversation rarely settles everything at once, and a follow-up session can pick up from wherever this one leaves off.

The Different Ways a Consultation Can Be Structured

Not every situation calls for the same format, so consultations here are offered in a few different structures depending on what you need from the conversation.

Some people want a brief introductory conversation to see whether this is useful at all. Others prefer a more focused session aimed at a single decision, a general energy check on how things currently feel, a structured overview based on your birth chart, or a plan for staying in touch over several follow-up conversations.

None of these formats is inherently better than another. They are simply suited to different situations and different comfort levels. The following sections describe each one in more detail.

You are also welcome to say you are not sure which format fits, and let the reader suggest one based on how you describe your situation, rather than choosing blind beforehand.

Intro Consultation: A Low-Pressure First Conversation

This is the simplest format available, meant for anyone who isn’t sure whether a psychic consultation is right for them at all. You describe your situation in general terms, and the reader responds with an honest first impression of it.

There is no expectation that this conversation resolves everything. It is meant to help you decide whether continuing, in any format, would be worthwhile for you, without any pressure attached to that decision either way.

Clarity Session: Focused on One Specific Question

A clarity session narrows in on a single decision or question rather than covering broad ground. It suits people who already know what they are trying to work through and want a focused conversation about exactly that.

The reader concentrates the entire consultation on that one question, working through it from a few different angles rather than moving on to unrelated topics that dilute the time available.

  • The forces currently shaping one specific decision
  • What is realistically within your control versus outside it

Energy Check: A General Read on How Things Currently Feel

An energy check is less about one specific question and more about a general read on your current situation, how things feel right now, and whether that matches what is actually happening around you.

It suits people going through a period of change who want a broader perspective rather than a single narrow answer to one question, especially when several things are shifting at once.

Birth-Chart Overview: A Structured Look at Timing

This format uses your birth details to build a structured overview of timing and tendencies, rather than an open-ended intuitive conversation. It suits people who prefer a more organized, reference-based approach to a reading.

The reader walks through what your chart suggests about the current period and connects it to whatever situation you have described, treating the chart as a reference point rather than a fixed script to follow.

  • Whether the current period favors action or patience
  • Recurring tendencies that show up across your chart

Follow-Up Plan: Staying in Touch Over Time

Some situations do not resolve in a single conversation. A follow-up plan sets up a loose structure for checking back in, after a decision has played out, or after enough time has passed to see whether an earlier impression held up.

This is not a subscription or a required commitment of any kind. It is simply an option for people who would rather revisit a situation deliberately than start over from scratch each time, especially when a decision unfolds gradually rather than all at once.

  • A reasonable interval for checking back in on a decision
  • What is worth watching for between now and then
  • How to describe any changes clearly at the next conversation
  • Whether a different format might suit a follow-up better than the original one

Whichever format you start with, none of them locks you in. Plenty of people begin with a brief intro consultation and, over time, settle into whichever structure actually fits how they think through decisions.
